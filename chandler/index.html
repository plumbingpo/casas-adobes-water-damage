How Chandler Homeowners Can Avoid Water Damage

Water damage looks sudden, but the causes are usually slow: a hose that aged, a seal that dried out, a drain that clogged a little more each month. For homeowners in Chandler, prevention is mainly a matter of knowing where to look.

Pipes and fittings top the list. Watch your water bill and your water pressure, because an unexplained rise in either can point to a hidden leak or stress on the pipes. Appliances are second. Run the dishwasher and the washer only while someone is in the house, and change their hoses before they show cracks. Third is the outside of the building. Rain gets in through damaged roofing, failed flashing, clogged gutters and ground that tilts toward the walls.

Going away for more than a few days? Turn the water off at the main, or ask someone to check the house. Casas Adobes Restoration is glad to talk through prevention with any homeowner in Chandler. If something does fail, calling quickly is what keeps the damage contained.

The first hour is when good intentions cause the most trouble. Here are the slip-ups we run into most on Chandler jobs, with the better option for each.

Do not use a household vacuum on water. It is an electric shock risk and it will ruin the machine; use towels and a mop. Restoration Challenges Chandler Homeowners Face

Ask anyone on our crew what fills the schedule in Chandler and you will hear the same six answers. None of them is rare, and each gets worse the longer the water sits.

Mold: Mold is usually the second chapter of a water problem that someone thought was finished. Fans and towels dry what you can see, while the wall cavity, subfloor and carpet pad stay damp underneath. Growth can begin within 24 to 48 hours, then spread quietly until you smell it or see spotting.

Hoses: Every sink, toilet and washer is fed by a flexible connector that holds full house pressure around the clock. A burst connector keeps spraying until somebody closes a valve, and that may take a long while in an empty house. Replacing old connectors and knowing where your shutoff sits are the cheapest protection available.

Pumps: In homes that have one, a sump pump works unnoticed for years and then quits on the one night it is needed. Stuck float switches, tripped breakers and burned-out motors are what we find most. That leaves the lowest level under a few inches of water, with flooring, wall bases and stored belongings soaked. Testing the pump now and then, and adding a battery backup, lowers the odds considerably.

Toilets: An overflowing toilet is the most common bathroom emergency we handle in Chandler. Clean water from the tank or supply is one thing, but water from the bowl after use is contaminated. Either way it moves under tile edges and vanity cabinets, so the floor needs to be checked with a meter.

Cooling: Few people think about the small pipe that carries condensation away from an AC system. A rusted pan or a plugged line lets that water run into nearby flooring or the ceiling below. Because it happens slowly, the damage in many Chandler homes is well along before a stain appears. Having the line flushed each year, before the cooling season, prevents most of these.

Flooding: When storm drains cannot keep up with a downpour, surface water heads for the lowest doorway it can find. It reaches ground-level rooms, garages and walkout lower levels on the downhill side before anything else. We pump it out, discard materials that cannot be sanitized and keep equipment running until moisture readings are back to normal.
